Ingredients

* * *

|     |        |                                                               |
| --- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 3   | cups   | dried pinto beans                                             |
| 1   | med    | white or yellow onion, chopped                                |
| 2-3 | cloves | garlic, chopped                                               |
| 1   | piece  | dried Kombu seaweed 1/2-inch (optional)                       |
| 1-2 | whole  | dried chipotle peppers or canned chipotles in adobo, to taste |
| 1   | tsp    | chili powder                                                  |
| 2   | tsp    | dried epazote\*                                               |
| 1   | tbsp   | olive oil                                                     |
| 1½  | tsp    | sea salt                                                      |

Cooking Instructions

* * *

1. Soak the beans in water, covered by 2 inches, for 8 hours or overnight.
2. Drain and rinse the beans in a colander a few times, then transfer to a large pot.
3. Add the onion, garlic, seaweed, chipotles, and spices.  Add water to cover the beans by 2 inches.
4. Bring to a boil an simmer over medium-low heat for about 1 hour, or until the beans are soft and cooked through.
5. Drain the beans, reserving 4 cups of the liquid.  Remove the seaweed.  Remove the chiles, or leave one in if spicier beans are desired.
6. Cool the beans for 15 minutes, then place in a food processor along with 1/2 cup of the liquid and process until smooth.  If desired, you may thin the beans with additional cooking liquid.
7. Return the pureed beans to the pot with the olive oil and salt.
8. Simmer over low to medium-low heat for 20 minutes to allow the flavors to blend.
9. Serve warm.

Refried beans keep refrigerated for 5 to 6 days or freeze well for several months.  For a quick meal or snack, spread cold beans on a corn tortilla and toast in a toaster oven for 1 to 2 minutes and top with “cheese” spread, guacamole, salsa, and/or hot sauce.

\*Note:  Epazote is an herb that can make beans more digestible, as well as adding a distinctive flavor.  Look for it in Mexican grocery stores or near the Mexican foods at the supermarket.  If you can’t find it, you can substitute 3 tbsp chopped fresh cilantro, stirred in just before serving.

_makes 7 cups, 8-10 servings_
